Tides lead, the storm slips between themPeak Wed about 11:30 AM; rain and wind land between peaks

In the Likely case, every daily high from Monday to Saturday reaches the minor flood level at SFO's shoreline, and Tuesday and Wednesday cross into moderate. The top is Wednesday around 11:30 AM, about 9.6 ft at SFO's shoreline, roughly 40 minutes after the official San Francisco gauge peaks. NWS forecasts that gauge at 8.6 ft on its own scale (8.7 ft here), a hair under its record from January 1983.

The NWS meteorologist who warned SFO's emergency manager on Saturday puts the water at about 9.9 ft against SFO's shoreline, where some seawall sections are as low as 8 ft. Heights there may use a different reference than these, so read his number as a cross-check, not a wall-by-wall comparison.

NWS's hourly grid for SFO has about 0.55 in of rain from Tuesday 3 PM to Wednesday morning, mostly Tuesday evening and overnight, with southerly gusts near 40 mph from Tuesday noon into the night. Both mostly land between Tuesday's and Wednesday's peaks. Wind-driven surge is the forecast's biggest unknown, which is what the High case covers.

SFO says drainage is cleared, pump stations are in "top operating condition", and sandbags and portable barriers are in high-risk areas. UC Davis's Fraser Shilling rates the chance of SFO flooding as very low, but says no runway escapes if water tops the wall.

How the estimate worksNOAA tide table plus the NWS forecast; ground heights from USGS lidar

Expected is NOAA's tide table at Oyster Point plus however far NWS expects the San Francisco gauge to run above its own table that day: 1.1 to 1.8 ft Monday to Saturday, including up to 1.6 ft of surge. Sunday, past the NWS forecast, assumes 1.2 ft, about where El Niño has held the bay for a month. Range is 0.3 ft either side; High case adds 0.3 ft, the margin by which storm days beat the forecast surge last week, per the Oakland Flood Week analysis.

SFO flood levels: minor 8.5 ft, moderate 9.4 ft, major 10.7 ft. These are NOAA's San Francisco thresholds, carried over as height above Oyster Point's average high tide. Expected is shown with a range of 0.3 ft either side.

Rain and wind are NWS's gridded forecast for SFO, in 3-hour blocks. Drains turns orange when rain falls while the bay sits a foot or more above its average high tide, so outfalls can't empty, and red when that happens at minor flood level or higher. NWS notes high tide can arrive up to 90 minutes off the predicted time.

All heights are in feet above NAVD88, a fixed survey reference. NOAA's tide tables count from each station's own low-water mark instead: at the San Francisco gauge the two are within 0.1 ft, but at Oyster Point the low-water mark sits about 0.6 ft lower, so this page reads about 0.6 ft below NOAA's SFO-area tide tables. Flood levels under the color bar: SFO's shoreline protection, 9.2 ft in a typical section of SFO's maintenance drawings; the Embarcadero promenade and roadway between the Ferry Building and Pier 14, 9.2 ft; China Basin Park on Mission Creek, 9.3 ft (Port of San Francisco flood risk profiles). The Port notes nuisance flooding and wave splash near Pier 14 already happen below that level.

Ground height on the map shades all land by height above sea level (USGS 3DEP, cells about 90 m across). Near the water, pink to purple marks ground that sits below the water level right now, shaded by how far below (pink under 1 ft, dark pink 1 to 2 ft, deep purple 2 ft or more), from the same lidar averaged into cells about 30 m across and compared with the water at the nearest gauge: Oyster Point from Candlestick south, the SF gauge elsewhere. It's a bathtub view. It ignores seawalls, levees, pumps and drains, so pink and purple ground inside the shoreline floods only if water gets over or through the shoreline and seawalls, and a wall thinner than a cell won't show. Heights are feet above NAVD88; forecast water is converted the same way (at Oyster Point, low-water datum sits about 0.57 ft below NAVD88). SFO's own maintenance drawings put existing shoreline protection at 9.2 ft in a typical section, and 1 m lidar sampled along 53 lines across SFO's bay edge puts about 20 of those crests below Wednesday's likely peak. Lidar can miss a thin wall, so treat that as a place to watch, not a forecast that water gets in.
